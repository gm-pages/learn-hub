The Two-Hour Moon

Why a geocentric Moon can run almost two hours ahead of, or behind, the one above you.

| Two Numbers

There are only two numbers on this page. The first is how fast the Moon moves: about 13.2 degrees a day, or 0.55 degrees an hour, so it covers one degree in about 1.8 hours. The second is how far parallax can move it: up to about 1.0 degree between the center of the Earth and a point on the surface, largest when the Moon is near the horizon and zero when it is overhead.

Put them together. If the real Moon over your head sits one degree away from where a geocentric calculation puts it, then the geocentric calculation is describing where the Moon will be, or was, about 1.9 hours away from the moment in question. The error is not in the clock. It is in the vantage point. But it reaches you as time.

| What Two Hours Means in Human Design

Two hours of Moon is a lot of Human Design. This is how long the Moon takes to cross each layer of a Gate:

Gate5.6250°10.2 hours

Base0.00521°34 seconds

Mean lunar motion. The Moon speeds up and slows down through the month, so real crossing times vary around these.

So a parallax shift of one degree is about one Line, 6 Colors, 37 Tones or 183 Bases of Moon. A geocentric Moon that is almost two hours out is not slightly out at the sub-line level. It is describing a different Color, a different Tone and a different Base, and quite possibly a different Line.

Which way?

Roughly speaking, a Moon in the eastern half of your sky reads slightly ahead of its geocentric position, a Moon in the western half reads slightly behind, and a Moon high overhead reads almost exactly where geocentric puts it. Two people in different cities at the same instant can therefore be out in opposite directions.

| Where It Shows Up

Your natal Moon

Your natal Moon is a transit that happened once. Whatever parallax applied at that instant, from that place, applies to your Moon activation in both Personality and Design. If the Moon was low in the sky when you were born, the difference between the two methods is at its largest.

The transiting Moon

Most people meet the Moon through transits, and the Moon changes Gate about every ten hours and Line about every hour and three-quarters. A calculation from the center of the Earth keeps those changes in step with a sky nobody stands under. A topocentric calculation keeps them in step with the sky above the place you are.

Reflectors and the lunar cycle

Reflectors live by the Moon's cycle more than any other Type, so they have the most reason to want the right Moon. A Moon that is a Line out is a Line out for everyone; a Reflector is the person most likely to feel it.

| Frequently Asked Questions

Is the geocentric Moon ahead or behind?
It depends on where the Moon is in your sky. Roughly, a Moon in the eastern half of your sky reads slightly ahead of its geocentric position, a Moon in the western half slightly behind, and a Moon high overhead almost exactly where geocentric says. That is why the same geocentric error can go either way for two people in different places.
Does this affect my natal Moon?
Yes. Your natal Moon is a transit that happened once, at the moment you were born. Whatever parallax applied to the Moon at that instant, from that place, applies to your natal Moon activation.
Does it affect the Sun's timing too?
Barely. The Sun's parallax is under 9 arc-seconds and the Sun moves about a degree a day, so the timing difference is a matter of minutes at the Base level and nothing at the Line level. The two-hour figure is a Moon figure.
What about Reflectors?
Reflectors live by the Moon more than any other Type, so they are the people this page is most about. A transiting Moon that is one Line out is one Line out for everyone, but a Reflector is the person most likely to notice. The right Moon, from the right place, is the whole point for a Reflector.
