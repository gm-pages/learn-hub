Topocentric Human Design

Geocentric vs Topocentric

Your chart has always known when you were born. Topocentric is the first Human Design calculation that also knows where.

| What This Is

Every Human Design chart is built from the positions of the planets at the moment of birth. What is rarely said is where those positions are measured from. Every chart you have ever seen, on any platform, measures them from the center of the Earth. That is what geocentric means.

Topocentric measures them from the point on the Earth's surface where you were born: your latitude, your longitude and your height above sea level. It is the same sky, the same planets, the same moment. The only thing that changes is the vantage point. Because the Moon is close to us, that change of vantage point moves the Moon by up to about one degree, which in Human Design is about one full Line.

Topocentric is available with a Genetic Matrix Plus subscription and above. Geocentric remains the default and the standard.

| Two Ways to Ask the Same Question

The question is always where was the Moon at the moment you were born? The two methods answer it from two different places.

Geocentric

Planet positions are calculated as seen from the center of the Earth, about 6,371 km below your feet. One vantage point for every person alive. Your birthplace is used for one thing only: to work out which time zone converts your birth time to universal time.

Topocentric

Planet positions are calculated as seen from your actual birthplace on the Earth's surface. Latitude and longitude decide where each planet sat in your sky; altitude adds the last few meters. Two people born at the same instant in different places get two different Moons.

The Bogotá and Munich Problem

Imagine two babies born at exactly the same instant, one in Bogotá and one in Munich. Under geocentric calculation their bodygraphs are identical, activation for activation, down to the last Base. Not similar. Identical. Human Design has always taught that the place of birth is part of the design, but with a geocentric calculation the place does nothing except set the clock. Topocentric is the calculation that lets the place actually enter the chart.

| Why It Matters More in Human Design Than in Astrology

Astrology reads the sky in whole degrees. A sign is 30 degrees wide, an aspect is allowed several degrees of orb, and a one-degree shift in the Moon changes almost nothing. That is why astrologers have never worried about parallax, and why the software Human Design inherited from astrology never bothered with it.

Human Design reads the same sky with a far finer instrument. Each Gate is divided into Lines, each Line into Colors, each Color into Tones, each Tone into Bases. The table shows how narrow those layers are and how quickly the Sun and Moon move through them.

The Moon's parallax averages about 0.95°, roughly 1 Line, 6 Colors, 37 Tones or 183 Bases. A shift that astrology can round away is, in Human Design, wider than an entire Line. That is the whole argument in one sentence: Human Design chose a microscope and has been reading it with the naked eye.

| What Can Move, and What Usually Does Not

The Moon is the body that moves

The Moon is close, about 384,400 km away, so the Earth's width is a meaningful fraction of the distance and the change of vantage point shows. Its position can differ by up to about 1.0° between the center of the Earth and a point on the surface. The shift is largest when the Moon is near the horizon and close to zero when it is overhead, which is why both the place and the time of day decide the result. In practice the Moon's Color, Tone and Base will very often differ between geocentric and topocentric, its Line fairly often, and its Gate when the Moon sits near a Gate boundary.

The Sun barely moves, so Profile holds

The Sun is so far away that its parallax is under 9 arc-seconds. That is far too small to move the Sun's Gate or Line, so your Profile, which is built from the Sun and Earth Lines, does not change under topocentric. It is, however, about half the width of a Base (47%), so the Sun's Base can flip when it sits close to a Base boundary. The deepest layer moves even on the steadiest body.

Inner planets sometimes, outer planets almost never

Mercury, Venus and Mars have parallaxes of tens of arc-seconds at most, so they can change Base or Tone when they sit close to a boundary. Jupiter and beyond move by less than a Base and can be treated as fixed.

The Lunar Nodes

The Nodes are not bodies. They are the points where the Moon's orbit crosses the ecliptic, a piece of orbital geometry rather than something seen from a place. On first principles they are expected to hold under topocentric. We report what the calculation engine measures rather than assume it, and the results will be published on this page.

The cascade, and why we say can, not will

Every Gate has two edge Lines, the 1st and the 6th. When an activation sits on an edge Line and the parallax shift nudges it across, it lands in the neighboring Gate. A new Gate can complete a channel, a new channel defines a Center, and a newly defined Center can change your Type or your Authority. Most charts do not have a Moon on a Gate edge, so most charts keep their Type. Some do. Topocentric can change Type or Authority; it does not will. Nobody knows which group they are in until they run both.

A stranger result is possible too: because Profile comes from the Sun, a person can keep their Profile and still change Type. Same costume, different engine.

| Frequently Asked Questions

Is my geocentric chart wrong?
No. Geocentric is the method Human Design has used since the beginning, it is what every other platform calculates, and it remains the default on Genetic Matrix. Topocentric adds one thing geocentric leaves out: the exact place on Earth you were born. For most people most activations stay the same. The question is whether any of yours sit close enough to a boundary to move.
Can topocentric change my Type or Authority?
It can, and for most people it does not. Type and Authority depend on which Gates are activated and which channels they complete. An activation only moves to a neighboring Gate when it already sits within the parallax shift of that Gate's edge, and the Sun, the body Profile is built on, barely moves at all. The Moon is the body that moves, by up to about one Line. Where the Moon happens to sit at a Gate edge, a channel and a Center can change, and with them Type or Authority. We say can, never will.
Why has nobody done this before?
Because in astrology it does not matter. Astrology reads the sky in whole degrees and 30-degree signs, so a shift of one degree is rounding error there, and the software Human Design inherited was astrology software. Human Design reads the same sky down to arc-seconds, sixty times finer than the arc-minutes astrology works in. The same shift that astrology can ignore crosses real Human Design boundaries.
Which method should I use?
Run both on the same birth data and look at what moves. If nothing moves, you have your answer and your chart is unchanged. If something moves, you have a real question to sit with. Do not mix outputs from the two methods in one reading.
Does topocentric apply to transits as well?
Yes, and in some ways it matters more there. A natal chart is one moment; transits are a stream of moments, and the Moon crosses a Line about every hour and three-quarters. Calculating the transiting Moon from where you actually are keeps its Gate and Line changes in step with the sky above you rather than with the center of the Earth.
Do I need my birth altitude?
It helps but it is the smallest part of the picture. Latitude and longitude do almost all of the work because they decide where in your sky each planet was. Genetic Matrix resolves altitude from the birth location automatically; you do not need to know it.
Is topocentric included with a free account?
Topocentric is available on Plus and above. Free accounts calculate geocentric charts, which is the Human Design standard.
