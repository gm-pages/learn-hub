The Altitude Factor

Amsterdam vs Mexico City: what height above sea level changes, and what it does not.

| Three Parts to "Where"

When we say a topocentric chart is calculated from where you were born, "where" has three parts: how far north or south you were (latitude), how far east or west (longitude), and how high above sea level (altitude). A geocentric chart uses none of them to place the planets. It uses your birthplace once, to find the time zone, and then calculates the sky from the center of the Earth.

This page is about the third part, altitude, because it is the one people ask about most and the one most often oversold. The honest answer is that altitude is real, it is included, and it is the smallest of the three.

| Amsterdam and Mexico City

Amsterdam

Roughly 2 m below sea level in parts, and about 52° north. A child born here on a winter evening has the Moon in a very different part of the sky from a child born in the tropics at the same instant.

Mexico City

About 2,240 m above sea level and about 19° north. Its observers stand more than two kilometers further from the center of the Earth than Amsterdam's do.

Let us take the two kilometers first. The Earth's radius is about 6,371 km, so 2,240 m of altitude moves an observer outward by about 0.035% of that radius. Parallax scales with that distance, so the Moon's parallax grows by the same fraction: at most about 1.3 arc-seconds, which is about 7% of one Base. For the Sun the altitude effect is 0.0031 arc-seconds, which is nothing.

What that means

Altitude on its own can tip the Moon's Base when the Moon already sits within a second or two of a Base boundary. It will not move a Tone, a Color or a Line by itself. It is a finishing touch, not the main event.

Now the part that does the work. Amsterdam and Mexico City are separated by about 33 degrees of latitude and about 104 degrees of longitude. At any given instant the Moon sits in a completely different part of the sky in each city: high over one, low near the horizon over the other, or below the horizon entirely. Parallax is largest near the horizon and vanishes overhead, so the difference between the two Moons can approach a full degree, and that is a Line. Latitude and longitude did that. Altitude added a second or two on top.

| Why Altitude Is Still In the Calculation

If the effect is that small, why take it at all? Because topocentric means calculating from where you actually were, and where you actually were includes how high you were. Rounding altitude to zero is a decision to be slightly wrong on purpose, and it is exactly the kind of decision geocentric made about latitude and longitude forty years ago, on a much bigger scale. The engine takes all three coordinates and lets the sky be whatever it was.

There is also a practical reason. Human Design's deepest layer, the Base, is under nineteen arc-seconds wide. At that resolution the last few meters are not decoration. They are part of the address.

A note on what altitude is not

Altitude does not bring you closer to the planets in any meaningful sense, and it does not change which planets are above your horizon. It changes your position by a couple of kilometers on a body several thousand kilometers across. That is all it does, and that is all this page claims for it.

“Latitude and longitude decide which sky you had. Altitude decides the last few meters of it.”

| Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to know my birth altitude?
No. Genetic Matrix resolves the altitude from the birth location you enter. If the place is right, the height is right.
Can altitude alone change my chart?
Only at the very deepest layer, and only rarely. The largest altitude difference between two inhabited places on Earth changes the Moon's position by a few arc-seconds at most, and a Base is 18.75 arc-seconds wide. So altitude can tip a Base that already sits on a boundary. It cannot move a Tone, a Color or a Line by itself.
Then why include it?
Because it is part of where you were born, and topocentric is the calculation that honors where you were born. Rounding it away would be a choice to be slightly wrong on purpose. The engine takes the true coordinates, all three of them, and lets the sky be whatever it was.
What matters more, altitude or latitude?
Latitude and longitude, by a very wide margin. They decide where the Moon sat in your sky, which decides how much parallax there was. Altitude is the last few meters of a calculation whose first few thousand kilometers are done by latitude and longitude.
